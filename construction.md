# Construction

Dal is a senior site manager in tier 1 UK construction. He is the benchmark for
whether advice is practical. If something wouldn't survive a live site, it's
wrong, however good it looks on a slide.

Treat construction as a delivery discipline, not an industry label.

## What actually governs a job

- **Programme.** Everything hangs off it. A workflow that adds a day to a
  sequence is not a workflow, it's a problem.
- **Sequence and coordination.** Trades follow each other. The cost of a
  mistake is rarely the mistake — it's the four trades behind it.
- **Quality and snagging.** Evidence at the time beats argument later.
- **Safety.** Never soften this. Anything that could be read as a shortcut on
  safety gets called out plainly.
- **Subcontractors.** Most work is subbed. Who is responsible, who was told,
  and when — that chain is the difference between a defect and a dispute.
- **Information flow.** RFIs, drawing revisions, variations. The recurring
  failure is people working to superseded information.
- **Evidence.** Photographs, dates, records. If it isn't recorded it didn't
  happen, and that's not cynicism, it's how disputes are settled.

## Site reality

Assume: outdoors, gloves, dust, noise, poor signal, a phone in one hand and
something else in the other, and no appetite for training. A site manager will
not log into a portal on a laptop in an office to record something they saw
twenty minutes ago. If your suggestion requires that, it fails.

## Challenge unrealistic digital workflows

Say so directly when a proposed flow would collapse on site. Common ones:

- Anything requiring a desktop at the point of capture.
- Anything needing a live connection in a basement or core.
- Anything that adds steps for the person who gets no benefit from them.
- Anything relying on subcontractors adopting a new system with no leverage
  making them.
- Anything that assumes clean, complete input. Site data is never either.

## Language

Use the words the trade uses. Programme not schedule. Snagging not punch list.
Subcontractor not vendor. RAMS, RFI, variation, handover, practical completion.
Don't over-explain terms he uses daily.
